# Modular Obstruction Lemma v0.1

Fix an odd positive fibre label \(a\), and let
\[
x_{a,k}=a2^k-1.
\]

## Lemma

Let \(r\) be an odd prime with \(r\nmid a\), and let
\[
d=\operatorname{ord}_r(2).
\]
If there exists \(k_0\) such that
\[
a2^{k_0}\equiv1\pmod r,
\]
then
\[
r\mid x_{a,k}
\iff
k\equiv k_0\pmod d.
\]
If no such \(k_0\) exists, the fibre has no divisibility obstruction from \(r\).

**Proof.** Since \(r\nmid a\),
\[
r\mid a2^k-1
\iff
2^k\equiv a^{-1}\pmod r.
\]
The powers of 2 modulo \(r\) form the cyclic subgroup generated by 2, with period \(d\). If \(a^{-1}\) lies in this subgroup, its exponent is unique modulo \(d\). Hence all and only solutions satisfy \(k\equiv k_0\pmod d\). \(\square\)

## Composite consequence

If additionally
\[
x_{a,k}>r
\]
at one of these exponents, then \(x_{a,k}\) is composite.

## Example: the \(a=3\) fibre

For \(r=5\),
\[
3\cdot2^1\equiv1\pmod5,
\qquad
\operatorname{ord}_5(2)=4.
\]
Therefore
\[
5\mid(3\cdot2^k-1)
\iff
k\equiv1\pmod4.
\]
The first point \(k=1\) is the prime \(5\) itself, while \(k=5\) gives
\[
3\cdot2^5-1=95=5\cdot19.
\]

This makes modular compositeness in each dyadic fibre a union of exact arithmetic progressions in the index \(k\).
